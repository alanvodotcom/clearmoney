An investment platform is the door you use to buy, hold, and sell assets—brokerage apps, full-service brokers, wrap platforms, or fund marketplaces. The platform is not the investment. A polished app can still sit on thin regulation, high fees, or a product shelf that nudges you toward complexity. Choose the pipe carefully; then choose the assets deliberately.

## What platforms typically offer

- Order placement for ASX shares and ETFs
- Access to managed funds or international markets
- Cash hubs, FX conversion, and sometimes margin lending
- Reporting for tax time and performance charts
- Research snippets, thematic collections, and push notifications

Features that help you execute a simple plan are useful. Features that encourage constant trading or leveraged products deserve scepticism.

## Fees you should add up in dollars

Look beyond “$0 brokerage” headlines. Common costs include:

- brokerage per trade or monthly subscription
- custody or platform administration fees
- FX spreads on dual-currency or international trades
- management fees inside ETFs and funds you hold
- inactivity fees, withdrawal fees, or premium data add-ons

A cheap trade that tempts weekly churn can cost more than a slightly dearer broker you barely use. Map costs against your real behaviour—monthly investing versus day trading cosplay.

## Safety and custody basics

Prefer platforms operated by identifiable Australian entities with clear AFSL arrangements for the services they provide. Understand how assets are held (for example CHESS sponsorship for ASX securities versus custodian omnibus structures) and what happens if the intermediary fails. Platform failure is not the same as an ADI deposit failure; protections differ.

Enable multi-factor authentication, use unique passwords, and be wary of apps sideloaded from ads. Cloned broker sites are a recurring scam pattern—type URLs carefully and read [how to spot a scam website](/topics/scams-safety/online-safety/how-to-spot-a-scam-website). Verify licences via [check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest).

Never give remote access to “support staff” who contacted you first. Real platforms do not need your SMS codes dictated over the phone.

## Product shelves and gentle nudges

Some apps surface CFDs, crypto, options, or thematic baskets beside vanilla ETFs. Proximity is not suitability. High-risk products can generate more revenue for the platform. Stick to the mandate in your [investing plan](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/develop-an-investing-plan). If you do not understand a product warning, skip it—see also [investment hype](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/dont-get-burned-by-investment-hype).

Comparison sites and lead-gen funnels may sell your details to multiple sales desks. That is annoying at best and a scam vector at worst ([using comparison websites](/topics/scams-safety/online-safety/using-comparison-websites)).

## Practical setup for long-term investors

1. Open only what you need (often one ASX broker is enough to start).
2. Link a dedicated funding account; avoid mixing rent money.
3. Turn off non-essential push alerts that trigger impulse trades.
4. Set a regular contribution schedule rather than waiting for “perfect” entries.
5. Download statements into your [tracking](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/track-your-investments) folder quarterly.

International access is convenient but adds FX cost and tax complexity. Start simple unless you have a clear reason to broaden.

## Switching later is normal

You are allowed to change brokers. Check transfer fees, corporate-action handling, and whether holdings move in-specie. Do not let sunk costs trap you in a high-fee wrap if a simpler arrangement now fits. Keep records of contract notes across platforms so tax history stays intact—see [investing and tax](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/investing-and-tax).

## Kids, joint logins, and household hygiene

If a partner shares access, agree rules about who can place trades and withdraw cash. Shared passwords without MFA invite accidents and abuse risks. For adult children using a parent’s brokerage “just to learn,” open a separate account instead—mixed ownership creates tax and estate headaches.

When you travel, know how to freeze the app if a phone is stolen. Platform security is part of investing hygiene, not an IT afterthought.

## What to do next

- List the assets you actually plan to buy, then shortlist platforms that support them without pushing extras.
- Add up brokerage, FX, and platform fees against your expected trade frequency.
- Verify the provider and enable strong account security; use [check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest).
- Align the account with a written [investing plan](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/develop-an-investing-plan) and diversified building blocks.
- Store statements and learn how to export tax reports before financial year end.
