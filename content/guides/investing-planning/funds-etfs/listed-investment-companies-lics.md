Listed investment companies (LICs) are companies listed on the share market whose main business is investing in a portfolio of assets. You buy shares in the LIC, not units in an open-ended fund. That closed-end structure creates quirks—especially discounts and premiums to net tangible assets (NTA)—that ETF investors sometimes miss.

## LIC mechanics in brief

A LIC raises capital and invests according to its mandate. The share price is set by supply and demand on the exchange. The portfolio has an NTA (or similar net asset measure) published regularly. If investors dislike the manager, fees, or liquidity, the share price can sit **below** NTA for long stretches. If enthusiasm runs hot, it can trade **above** NTA.

Open-ended managed funds and many ETFs are designed so transacting happens near asset value (with spreads and costs). LICs do not redeem your shares at NTA on demand—you sell to another market participant.

## Why discounts appear—and why “cheap” can stay cheap

Discounts may reflect:

- thick management fees or performance fee structures
- concentrated or opaque portfolios
- poor historical capital allocation (for example, value-destructive capital raisings)
- low liquidity in the LIC shares themselves
- market moods toward active management

Buying a 15% discount is not an automatic 15% gain. Discounts can widen. Some LICs address gaps with buy-backs or capital management; others do little. Read director commentary with scepticism and look at actions.

## Comparing LICs with ETFs and unlisted funds

| Feature | Typical LIC | Typical ETF / open fund |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Company shares | Units / ETF securities |
| Exit | Sell on market | Redeem or sell near NAV |
| Price vs assets | Discount/premium common | Usually tight to NAV |
| Capital | Often closed-end | ETF creation/redemption; fund applications |

Active LICs compete with low-cost index ETFs on after-fee results—a tough race. See [ETFs](/topics/investing-planning/funds-etfs/exchange-traded-funds-etfs) and [choosing a managed fund](/topics/investing-planning/funds-etfs/choosing-a-managed-fund).

## Dividends, franking, and capital moves

Many LICs emphasise dividend history. Dividends can be attractive and sometimes franked, but they are not guaranteed. Paying steady dividends while trading at a wide discount does not automatically fix the capital account. Capital raisings, options issues, and DRPs can affect existing holders—read notices.

Tax treatment of dividends and capital gains depends on your situation; keep records and get personal tax advice when amounts matter.

## Research checklist before you buy a ticker

- Mandate and allowed asset ranges
- Management agreement and fee schedule
- NTA reporting frequency and valuation policies for unlisted holdings
- Share liquidity (average daily volume)
- Discount/premium history across years
- Board independence and alignment (skin in the game)

Illiquid LICs with complex unlisted assets can behave more like alternatives than like a simple share ETF—price gaps may not reflect what you could realise if the portfolio were sold tomorrow.

## Hype and lookalike risks

New LICs floated into hot markets can list with fanfare and later drift to discounts. Treat IPO-like enthusiasm carefully ([what is an IPO?](/topics/investing-planning/shares/shares-what-is-an-ipo)). Fake “listed fund” offers on unlicensed platforms are scam territory—verify brokers and products ([check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest)).

## When a buy-back is not a free lunch

Some LICs announce on-market buy-backs to narrow discounts. That can help—if done at sensible prices and not as a distraction from weak investment performance. Read whether buy-backs are routine capital management or a one-off PR move. Your edge, if any, still depends on what the portfolio owns and what you paid relative to lasting value, not a single announcement headline.

## What to do next

- Decide whether you specifically want closed-end dynamics—or whether an ETF/open fund is simpler for your goal.
- Compare LIC share price with reported NTA and ask why any gap exists.
- Read fees and capital-management history before yield marketing.
- Check liquidity so you can exit without moving the price dramatically.
- Hold LICs only as a deliberate active choice inside a diversified plan—not by accident of a tip.
