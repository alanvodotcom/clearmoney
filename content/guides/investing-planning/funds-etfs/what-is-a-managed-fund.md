A managed fund pools money from many investors and buys a portfolio according to a stated strategy—Australian shares, global bonds, balanced mixes, and countless variations. You typically hold units, not the underlying shares directly. Professional management and diversification are the pitch; fees and strategy risk are the trade-offs. This is general information, not a recommendation to buy any fund.

## Units, prices, and applications

When you invest, you buy units at a price linked to the fund’s net assets (often calculated each business day). When you withdraw, units are redeemed under the fund’s rules. Unlike shares on the ASX, you usually transact with the fund (or via a platform) rather than with another investor during continuous trading—though [ETFs](/topics/investing-planning/funds-etfs/exchange-traded-funds-etfs) provide a listed cousin with different mechanics.

Distributions can include income and realised capital components. You may pay tax on distributions even if you reinvest them—keep statements for lodgement and seek personal tax help if needed.

## What “managed” does and does not mean

A manager selects securities (or tracks an index), handles corporate actions, and reports. Management does **not** mean your capital is guaranteed, protected by the Financial Claims Scheme like an ADI deposit, or immune from market falls. Equity funds can drop sharply; credit funds can impair; property funds can freeze withdrawals in stress.

Read the Product Disclosure Statement for investment objective, asset ranges, risks, fees, and liquidity terms. The PDS is dull on purpose.

## Fee awareness in dollars

Ongoing management fees, contribution/withdrawal spreads, and occasional performance fees all reduce what you keep. Small percentage gaps compound into large dollar gaps over decades—try [fee drag](/tools/fee-drag). Cheap is not always best if a fund fails its job, but expensive is not a quality stamp.

Platform fees may sit on top when you hold funds inside a wrap or super product. Add the layers before celebrating a “bargain” management cost.

## Types you will meet

- **Index funds** — aim to match a market
- **Active funds** — aim to outperform a benchmark
- **Multi-asset / balanced** — mix growth and defensive assets
- **Single-sector** — one asset class only
- **Specialist** — smaller niches, often higher cost and risk

Related structures include LICs ([listed investment companies](/topics/investing-planning/funds-etfs/listed-investment-companies-lics)) and more complex absolute-return vehicles ([hedge funds](/topics/investing-planning/funds-etfs/hedge-funds)). Names blur; documents clarify.

## Liquidity and gates

Most mainstream funds process withdrawals regularly, but some asset types (unlisted property, private credit, certain alternatives) can delay or limit redemptions when many investors exit together. If you might need cash soon, prioritise liquidity in the PDS—not the brochure photo.

## Where managed funds sit in a plan

Funds are tools for implementing [diversification](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/diversification) without picking every security yourself. They still need a purpose inside an [investing plan](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/develop-an-investing-plan). Choosing among them is a separate skill—see [choosing a managed fund](/topics/investing-planning/funds-etfs/choosing-a-managed-fund).

Watch for unlicensed “managed” schemes promising fixed high returns—a perennial [investment scam](/topics/scams-safety/financial-scams/investment-scams) pattern. Verify via [check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest).

## Responsible entity and custody—why the boring names matter

Australian managed funds sit under legal structures with a responsible entity and custodial arrangements described in the PDS. You are not depositing with a bank. Understanding who holds assets and how unit pricing is calculated will not make markets calm, but it stops you from confusing a fund balance with an ADI deposit covered by the Financial Claims Scheme.

If a salesperson cannot explain who the responsible entity is, slow down.

## What to do next

- Read a sample PDS focusing on objective, risks, fees, and withdrawal rules.
- Decide whether an unlisted fund or an ETF better fits how you like to invest.
- Estimate cost impact with [fee drag](/tools/fee-drag) before committing large balances.
- Place any fund inside a written plan and review annually via [track your investments](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/track-your-investments).
- Verify unfamiliar operators through [check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest).
