“Free” investment seminars and wealth lunches are often sales funnels. Education is the bait. The close is a costly course, mentorship, property spruik, or high-risk product. Your job is to leave with your chequebook closed.

If you signed something under pressure and fear financial harm, read [/urgent](/urgent) and seek free financial counselling if debt stress follows.

## The typical arc

1. Ads promising secrets banks hate
2. Free event with food or a “limited seat” tease
3. Charismatic stories and testimonials
4. Soft-sell to a second, paid event
5. Hard-sell: courses, software, property deals, or introductions to “partners”
6. Finance options so you can buy education you cannot afford

Affinity marketing—targeting a cultural, religious, or professional community—raises the emotional stakes. Scammers know trust travels inside groups ([investment hype](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/dont-get-burned-by-investment-hype)).

## Questions that puncture the show

- Are you licensed to give personal advice—and for which products? ([advisers register](/topics/investing-planning/advice/financial-advisers-register))
- What is the total cost including upsells?
- Can I take documents home unread for 48 hours?
- What are the failure cases—not just winners on stage?
- Who pays you when you recommend a property or software partner?

No straight answers? Exit. You do not owe politeness to a high-pressure room.

## Property and SMSF spruiks

Be especially wary of seminars that push geared property, options strategies, or SMSF property “systems” as passive riches ([SMSFs and property](/topics/investing-planning/property/smsfs-and-property), [timeshares](/topics/investing-planning/property/timeshares)). Independent research and licensed advice beat a ballroom pitch.

## After you escape—or if you already paid

Cooling-off rights may apply to some purchases—check consumer law for your contract type quickly. Complain in writing. If the “education” funnels into a fake trading platform, switch into scam response mode ([investment scams](/topics/scams-safety/financial-scams/investment-scams)).

## Two-day intensives and mastermind upsells

After the free night comes the $2,000–$20,000+ workshop, then a mastermind, then “deal flow” access. Each stage normalises spending. Calculate total cost of the ladder before day one, including travel and lost wages.

Ask graduates—independently, not the ones planted near the stage—what their net results were after two years. Anecdotes of Lamborghinis are not cohort data.

## Recording, contracts, and cooling off

Take your own notes. Be wary if photography of slides is banned while you are asked to sign quickly. Use cooling-off rights where they apply; diary the deadline the same evening.

Pay with methods that preserve dispute options when possible. Avoid draining offset accounts or redraw facilities for education that has not proven value.

## Community venues do not equal endorsement

Hiring a club, temple, or sports club room is not an endorsement by that community. Scammers rent respectability. Leaders who host should demand licensing proof before providing a microphone.

## Refunds, chargebacks, and complaints

If you paid under pressure, contact your card issuer or bank promptly about dispute options and write to the seminar company cancelling. Lodge complaints with consumer affairs and AFCA where a financial firm is involved. Parallel paths beat waiting politely for a goodwill refund that never arrives.

## Kids and partners as props

Some seminars encourage bringing family to increase social pressure. Decide attendance rules beforehand: no partners as human shields for the close, no same-night purchases, no sharing of bank login screens with “helpers” in the room.

## Spotting planted testimonials

Audience members who speak first, praise aggressively, and steer others toward the back-of-room signup table may be plants. Ask how they know the presenter and whether they are paid. Silence or vagueness answers the question.

## What to do next

- Decline free seminar invites that use urgency and guarantees.
- If you attend for research, take a sceptical friend and leave before the paid close.
- Never buy on the night; sleep on any offer.
- Verify licences independently ([check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest)).
- Report misleading seminars through consumer and scam channels; use [/urgent](/urgent) if you are already in deep.
