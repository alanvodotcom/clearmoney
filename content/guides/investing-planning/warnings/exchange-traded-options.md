Exchange traded options (ETOs) are listed contracts that give the right—but not always the obligation—to buy or sell an underlying share or index at a strike price before or at expiry, depending on the style. Brokers market them as flexible tools. Without a clear plan they become expensive lottery tickets or hidden leverage.

Feeling rushed into an “options income” course after a seminar? Slow down—[investment hype](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/dont-get-burned-by-investment-hype) often sells complexity first.

## Rights, obligations, and premium

Buyers pay a premium for rights. Sellers (writers) collect premium and take on obligations that can require posting collateral or delivering stock. American-style equity options can be exercised early; index options are often cash-settled. Those details change risk more than a colourful payoff diagram suggests.

Owning shares ([how to buy and sell shares](/topics/investing-planning/shares/how-to-buy-and-sell-shares)) is still the simpler path for most long-term goals.

## Time decay is not a bug—it is the product

Options lose value as expiry approaches if the market does not move enough in your favour (all else equal). Strategies that look neat on day one can bleed every quiet week. “Theta income” sellers can still face large losses when markets gap; collecting small premiums is not free money.

Compare any leveraged or derivative idea with the honesty test in [borrowing to invest](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/borrowing-to-invest): can you afford the bad path?

## Covered calls are still a trade-off

Writing calls against shares you own can generate income and also cap upside if the stock rallies through the strike. That may be fine—or it may sell your winners cheaply. There is no universal “best.” Read assignment and tax timing consequences before copying a YouTube template.

Naked short options and complex multi-leg spreads need margin, monitoring, and exit rules most beginners do not maintain.

## Retail funnels that oversell certainty

Guaranteed monthly income, “zero risk hedges,” and private mentoring upsells are common. Ask for independent verification of *student* results after fees and slippage. Offshore apps that wrap option-like payoffs inside CFDs inherit CFD problems ([CFDs](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/contracts-for-difference-cfds)).

Verify Australian licence status yourself ([check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest)). Scam shops still impersonate brokers ([investment scams](/topics/scams-safety/financial-scams/investment-scams)).

## Liquidity, spreads, and far-dated dreams

Illiquid strikes have wide spreads—you pay to enter and exit. Deep out-of-the-money lottery calls are cheap for a reason: most expire worthless. Treating them as a savings plan confuses hope with probability.

## When options can make sense (narrowly)

Hedging a concentrated employee shareholding, or implementing a written plan with a licensed adviser, is different from day-trading weekly expiries on margin. If you cannot explain max loss, margin, and expiry in plain English before clicking buy, you are not ready.

## Practice without funding heroics

Paper trade if you must learn mechanics—then decide whether the complexity earns a place beside a diversified core. Many investors never need ETOs and still meet their goals.

If you proceed, start with defined-risk long options only, tiny size, and a written max-loss. Add multi-leg spreads only after you have closed several live trades without surprise assignment drama. Keep a journal of why you entered—not just the payoff sketch.

Broker education modules are useful for definitions; they are not a green light to sell naked premium after one weekend.
## What to do next

- Learn payoff, premium, and assignment *before* funding multi-leg strategies.
- Treat “guaranteed options income” pitches as marketing until proven otherwise.
- Prefer liquid strikes and defined-risk sizes if you proceed at all.
- Keep your long-term wealth in a boring [investing plan](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/develop-an-investing-plan) first.
- Report fake broker platforms via [report an investment scam](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/report-an-investment-scam); use [/urgent](/urgent) if withdrawals are blocked.
- Revisit position size after any large market move—yesterday’s “small” premium can become today’s forced sale.
