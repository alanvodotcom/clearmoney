Futures are standardised contracts to buy or sell an asset—or settle in cash—at a set price on a set date. Farmers, miners, and fund managers use them to hedge. Retail ads often skip that context and sell leveraged speculation on indexes, metals, or oil.

If an unsolicited caller is pushing futures or “commodity accounts,” treat it as a sales and scam check first—[/urgent](/urgent) if money already moved.

## What you actually hold

A futures position is an obligation (or a cash-settled stand-in), not share ownership. Exchanges and clearing houses sit in the middle for listed contracts; that infrastructure is not the same as an offshore CFD shop’s terms. Still, you face margin calls, daily mark-to-market moves, and the risk of losing more than your initial deposit when leverage is high.

Owning an ETF that tracks an index is a different product with clearer long-horizon behaviour for most households ([diversification](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/diversification)).

## Margin is not a free loan

Initial and variation margin keep the clearing system whole. When prices move against you, you post more cash—or the broker closes you out. “Small deposit controls a large notional” is marketing language for concentrated risk. Compare that honestly with [borrowing to invest](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/borrowing-to-invest): leverage magnifies mistakes either way.

Overnight and rollover costs matter if you hold past nearby expiries. Rolling a losing speculative view repeatedly is how accounts quietly empty.

## Who futures are built for

Commercial hedgers offset real inventory or revenue risk. Speculators provide liquidity but need risk systems, capital, and time. A weekend course does not put you in the first group. If your goal is long-term wealth, listed futures are optional complexity—not a required skill.

Retail products that *feel* like futures but are OTC CFDs inherit CFD problems instead ([CFDs](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/contracts-for-difference-cfds)).

## Expiry, rolls, and surprise delivery language

Contracts expire. Staying in a view means rolling to a later month—often at a cost. Misreading delivery versus cash settlement can create operational stress near expiry. Read the contract specifications and your broker’s close-out rules before size grows.

## Education funnels and signal rooms

Commodity “academies,” Telegram oil tips, and gold doom channels often sell subscriptions harder than they teach risk. Demand audited personal results after fees—not lifestyle footage. Platforms that block withdrawals or invent taxes are following the [investment scam](/topics/scams-safety/financial-scams/investment-scams) playbook; verify licences ([check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest)).

## Currency and commodity headlines as emotional bait

News about inflation, wars, or rate decisions spikes retail interest in oil and gold futures. Emotional timing predicts overtrading. If sleep and cash buffers are shaky, delay any leveraged product—the urge usually fades when life stabilises.

## Safer ways to express a view

Prefer unleveraged funds or shares sized so a wrong call hurts pride, not rent. If you truly need institutional-style hedges for a business, speak with licensed advisers and your bank—not a social-media scalper. Related caution stories: [forex trading](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/forex-trading).

Before any futures-like product, write down: underlying, expiry, margin required for a 5% adverse move, and how you will exit. If you cannot fill that card, you are not ready to size a live order.

Treat “micro” contracts as still leveraged until you can explain variation margin without a brochure. Small ticket size does not remove the close-out risk.
## What to do next

- Default to “no retail futures speculation” unless you can explain margin calls and expiry in your own words and afford a total loss of the trading stake.
- Hang up on cold commodity-account pitches.
- Confirm whether an offer is exchange-traded futures or a CFD lookalike before depositing.
- Report blocked-withdrawal platforms via [report an investment scam](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/report-an-investment-scam).
- Redirect curiosity toward a simple [investing plan](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/develop-an-investing-plan) without leverage.
