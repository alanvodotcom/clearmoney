Prediction markets let you buy and sell contracts that pay out based on whether an event happens—an election result, a sports score, a company announcement. Platforms pitch them as “crowd wisdom” and “hedging.” For most Australians they behave like short-horizon betting with a financial wrapper.

If someone is pressuring you to fund an offshore prediction app after a cold contact, pause and use [/urgent](/urgent).

## Contracts on headlines, not ownership

You are not buying a share of a company or a unit in a fund. You hold a contract whose value swings with odds and news flow. Settlement depends on the platform’s rules, oracles, and willingness to pay. When the market is thin, prices can jump on a single large trade or a rumour.

That is closer to a sportsbook than to [building an investing plan](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/develop-an-investing-plan).

## Where the edge usually sits

Platforms and market makers earn spreads, fees, and sometimes the other side of uninformed flow. Casual traders react to social-media certainty; professionals price information faster than a lunch-break tip. Early “wins” on easy events are a classic hook—the same pattern shows up in [binary options](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/binary-options) funnels.

Ask: am I paying to express a political or sports opinion with money I need, or am I pretending this is portfolio construction?

## Licensing, geography, and grey apps

Australian product rules for event contracts and similar products can be strict. Offshore apps that court locals with crypto deposits, gift-card funding, or “no ID” onboarding are waving a red flag. Verify any firm on official registers yourself ([check before you invest](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/check-before-you-invest)) and cross-check [investor alerts](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/investor-alert-list).

Clone sites that steal branding of a known venue are common. Type URLs yourself ([scam websites](/topics/scams-safety/online-safety/how-to-spot-a-scam-website)).

## Ambiguous events and dispute traps

Contracts live or die on definitions: what counts as “occurred,” which data source wins, and what happens if an event is postponed. Ambiguous wording lets platforms delay or cancel payouts. Read the resolution rules before any deposit—not after a disputed election night.

If support asks for remote access or seed phrases to “unlock winnings,” you are in a scam ([investment scams](/topics/scams-safety/financial-scams/investment-scams)).

## Leverage and lookalike products

Some venues wrap prediction-style bets inside CFDs or crypto tokens. Leverage turns a wrong call into a margin wipe. Treat leveraged event products with the same caution as [CFDs](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/contracts-for-difference-cfds)—most people building long-term wealth never need them.

## Social proof and “insider” channels

Private Discord rooms selling “locked” prediction picks monetise hope the same way [investment hype](/topics/investing-planning/warnings/dont-get-burned-by-investment-hype) seminars do. Screenshots of green P&L are marketing, not audited track records of *your* account after fees and withdrawals.

Celebrity deepfakes and “sure thing” election trades are advertising, not analysis. If the pitch needs urgency (“odds close in an hour”), you are being sold speed, not insight.

## A healthier use of curiosity

Follow news without staking rent money. If you want market exposure, prefer ordinary diversified investments sized without event-bet urgency. If you already deposited and cannot withdraw, document everything and follow [report an investment scam](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/report-an-investment-scam).

Keep a simple rule: no new speculative apps while you are short on sleep, cash buffer, or time to read the resolution rules end to end.
## What to do next

- Assume prediction markets are gambling unless you fully understand fees, resolution rules, and total loss of the stake.
- Prefer apps that only take crypto or gift cards for “event trading”—then refuse them.
- Verify licences and type URLs yourself before any deposit.
- Delete tip-seller chats that mock caution.
- Put the same energy into a plain [investing plan](/topics/investing-planning/how-to-invest/develop-an-investing-plan) if wealth-building is the real goal.
- Re-read resolution rules after any platform update; definitions change more often than ads admit.
