A productive advice relationship feels like structured teamwork: you supply honest facts and priorities; the adviser supplies process, options, and documented recommendations; together you implement and review. It should not feel like a friendship cult, a secrecy pact, or a product conveyor belt.

## The usual arc of an engagement

1. **Discovery** — goals, dependents, assets, debts, insurance, risk capacity
2. **Strategy** — trade-offs explained in plain language
3. **Recommendations** — written personal advice with reasons
4. **Implementation** — applications, switches, portfolio changes you approve
5. **Review** — scheduled check-ins when markets or life move

Skip discovery and you get generic product pushes. Skip reviews and ongoing fees lose their point. See [general and personal financial advice](/topics/investing-planning/advice/general-and-personal-financial-advice) for why documentation matters.

## How to be a good client without being passive

Bring organised statements, tax returns if relevant, super balances, and a short list of decisions you need. Say what success looks like in concrete terms (“retire debt-free by 60,” “insure income adequately,” “invest windfall without freezing”). Disclose debts and spending realities; hidden constraints produce unsuitable advice.

You can challenge recommendations. Ask what alternatives were considered and what could go wrong. “Because the research desk likes it” is not enough.

## Boundaries that keep the relationship healthy

- Decisions remain yours—advisers recommend; you authorise
- Authorities to operate accounts should be limited and understood
- Communication preferences (email summaries after calls) reduce he-said-she-said
- Scope changes (new topics) may change fees—agree before work expands

If you feel rushed to sign, pause. Good advisers can live with your cooling-off instinct.

## Reviews that do work versus theatre

A useful review revisits goals, contributions, insurance adequacy, estate nominees, fee value, and whether the portfolio still matches the brief. A theatrical review only recaps last quarter’s returns and suggests a new product. Ask for the agenda in advance.

Ongoing fees should map to ongoing service ([financial advice costs](/topics/investing-planning/advice/financial-advice-costs)). If service thins out, say so early—or leave cleanly.

## When to seek a second opinion

Major irreversible steps—gearing, SMSF setup, whole-of-life insurance replacement, concentrated alternatives—may justify a second authorised adviser reviewing the file. That is not disloyalty; it is risk management for large commitments. Verify any new party ([financial advisers register](/topics/investing-planning/advice/financial-advisers-register)).

## Spotting drift into misconduct or scams

Pressure to move money to offshore platforms, install remote access, or hide activity from a partner is outside normal advice. Contact [/urgent](/urgent) pathways if needed and read [investment scams](/topics/scams-safety/financial-scams/investment-scams). Licensed advice problems that are not scams still have complaint routes ([problems with a financial adviser](/topics/investing-planning/advice/problems-with-a-financial-adviser)).

## Ending or pausing without chaos

Give notice according to your agreement, revoke authorities, and decide whether holdings stay put while you rethink. Implement changes in a planned sequence so tax and transaction costs do not spike from panic. Keep all SOAs for your records even after you leave.

## Joint clients and family complexity

Couples should decide whether meetings are joint, how disagreements are recorded, and what happens if one person wants to leave the engagement. Blended families and business partners need extra clarity about whose goals drive recommendations. Ask the adviser how they handle conflicts between joint clients—good process beats awkward silence later.

Bring power-of-attorney or carer arrangements into the open early if someone else may need to speak with the adviser on your behalf.

## What to do next

- Agree scope, fees, and review cadence in writing before implementation begins.
- Organise your documents so discovery is accurate the first time.
- Require plain-English trade-offs for every major recommendation.
- Diary review dates and hold the advice team to the service you pay for.
- Use [choosing a financial adviser](/topics/investing-planning/advice/choosing-a-financial-adviser) standards if the fit feels wrong early.
