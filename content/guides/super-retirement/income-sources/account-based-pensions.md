An account-based pension (ABP) keeps your super invested while you draw regular payments in retirement. You choose how much to take above a legislated minimum, how often payments arrive, and—within fund rules—which investment option holds the balance. That flexibility is the main reason most Australians with substantial super choose an ABP over a full lump sum. It is also why you carry the risk that markets, inflation, or your own spending could empty the account before you die.

This guide explains how ABPs work, how they interact with tax and the Age Pension, and where to stress-test your plan. Rules and caps change—confirm current figures with your fund, the ATO, and Services Australia before you act. ClearMoney offers general information, not personal financial advice.

## What an account-based pension does

When you meet a condition of release—typically reaching preservation age and retiring, or turning 65—you can move all or part of your super into retirement phase and start an ABP. The fund pays you an income stream while the remainder stays invested. Unlike a lifetime annuity, you can usually change payment amounts (subject to minimums), switch investment options, and leave unused capital to beneficiaries.

Most people start an ABP rather than taking everything as cash. See [What happens to your super when you retire](/topics/super-retirement/plan-retirement/what-happens-to-your-super-when-you-retire) for the broader picture. If you are weighing a one-off withdrawal instead, read [Super lump sum](/topics/super-retirement/income-sources/super-lump-sum) first.

## Setting your payment amount

Legislation sets a **minimum** annual drawdown that rises with age brackets. You must take at least that percentage of your account balance each financial year. You can take more, but every extra dollar is a bet against longevity.

Choose a payment frequency that matches your bills—fortnightly, monthly, or quarterly. Some funds let you take ad hoc amounts between scheduled payments. Ask your fund for the current minimum percentage table for your age; these percentages are updated by government instruments from time to time.

Use the [retirement planner](/tools/retirement-planner) to sketch whether your chosen payment rate might last through a long life and a mediocre market decade. The tool is a model, not a guarantee.

## Transfer balance cap and limits

There is a lifetime cap on how much you can move into retirement phase (the transfer balance cap). Amounts above the cap may need to stay in accumulation phase or be managed differently. The cap is indexed in $100,000 steps—verify the current figure on the ATO website before large conversions.

Exceeding the cap can trigger excess transfer balance tax and require commutation of some pension balances. If you hold multiple super accounts or an SMSF, coordination matters. Your fund or tax agent can help you map total retirement-phase balances.

## Tax treatment at 60 and beyond

For many people aged 60 and over, payments from a taxed super fund—including an ABP—are tax-free to the member. Earnings inside a retirement-phase pension are generally tax-exempt, subject to the transfer balance cap. Exceptions exist for untaxed funds, taxable components accessed under 60, and certain death-benefit scenarios.

Do not assume your neighbour's tax outcome applies to you. Confirm your fund type, components, and age with the ATO or a registered tax agent. [Retirement income and tax](/topics/super-retirement/manage-retirement/retirement-income-and-tax) covers how ABP payments sit alongside other income.

## Combining with Age Pension

An ABP affects Centrelink means tests differently depending on your age and how the income stream is assessed. Super in accumulation is treated one way; account-based pensions may be subject to deeming on the asset value and income rules on withdrawals. Getting this wrong can mean overpayment debts later.

If you expect to claim or already receive the Age Pension, read [Super and the Age Pension](/topics/super-retirement/plan-retirement/super-and-the-age-pension) and tell Centrelink when your super structure changes. Report promptly when you start, stop, or significantly alter pension payments.

## When flexibility becomes a risk

Account-based pensions fail quietly. Sequence risk—large market falls in the first years of retirement combined with steady withdrawals—can permanently shrink what remains. Inflation erodes fixed nominal payments unless you adjust them upward. Behaviour matters: many households spend more in good years and forget to tighten after a bad one.

Mitigations include holding one to three years of essential spending in cash or defensive assets, reviewing payments annually rather than reacting to headlines, and pairing an ABP with Age Pension or a partial annuity for essential floor income. [Make your money last in retirement](/topics/super-retirement/manage-retirement/make-your-money-last-in-retirement) walks through pacing rules in more detail.

## Choosing a payment size without guesswork

Start from your essential monthly spend, subtract Age Pension and other certain income, then set the ABP to cover the gap—plus a little discretionary room if the balance can bear it. If the minimum drawdown already exceeds what you need, take the minimum and leave the rest invested (confirm with your fund how mid-year adjustments work).

Couples sometimes open two pensions with different investment mixes: one defensive for near-term payments, one growth-tilted for later decades. That is optional complexity; a single well-buffered pension is enough for many people.

## Reversionary beneficiaries and death

Ask whether your pension can be reversionary to a spouse so payments continue without a full restart. Binding nominations still matter for residual balances. When a partner dies, Centrelink and tax settings can change quickly—diary a review within a month of any death benefit event.

## Fees in pension phase

Administration and investment fees continue. A cheap accumulation product is not automatically cheap as a pension. Compare pension-product PDSs specifically, including any advice fee trails you may have forgotten about.


## What to do next

- Ask your fund for the current minimum drawdown percentage for your age and a payment illustration including fees.
- Stress-test a lower return assumption in the [retirement planner](/tools/retirement-planner).
- Check transfer balance cap headroom on the ATO site if you hold large balances or multiple pensions.
- Tell Centrelink if you receive or plan to claim the Age Pension once your ABP starts.
- Set a yearly payment review date—your birthday is easy to remember.
