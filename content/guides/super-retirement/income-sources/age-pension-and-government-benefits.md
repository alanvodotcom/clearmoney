The Age Pension is a means-tested payment for eligible older Australians. For many retirees it forms the floor of retirement income—sometimes the entire floor. Rates, asset and income thresholds, deeming rules, and supplements change, often each year in March and September. Treat any dollar figure you heard at a barbecue as outdated until you confirm it on Services Australia.

This guide covers eligibility basics, how means tests work in practice, related concessions, and how to apply without creating debts. ClearMoney offers general information only—not personal advice. Always verify current rules on official sites.

## Who can claim and when

You generally need to meet three broad tests: Age Pension age, residency rules, and income and assets tests. Age Pension age is currently 67 for people born on or after 1 January 1957. Earlier birth cohorts had lower ages—check your date-of-birth group on Services Australia.

Residency requirements include living in Australia and being an Australian resident for a minimum period. Some visa holders and newer arrivals face waiting periods or exclusions. If you lived overseas, gather evidence of Australian residence before lodging.

You do not need to be fully retired to claim, but employment income affects your rate through the income test and work bonus settings.

## How means tests work

Centrelink assesses your income and your assets separately. You receive the lower resulting rate—not an average of the two. Income includes deemed earnings on financial investments, rent, wages, and some pensions. Assets include bank accounts, shares, super (depending on age and product type), investment property, and personal effects above thresholds.

Homeowners and non-homeowners face different asset test limits. Your principal home is generally exempt from the assets test, but it still matters for aged care and some concession cards. Deeming assumes financial investments earn a set rate regardless of actual returns—a low-return year can still count as deemed income.

## Super and your pension rate

Super in accumulation phase is usually assessable once you reach Age Pension age. Account-based pensions and other income streams may be assessed under specific rules including deeming on the asset value. The interaction is detailed enough to deserve its own read: [Super and the Age Pension](/topics/super-retirement/plan-retirement/super-and-the-age-pension).

Large super balances do not automatically disqualify you—many part-pensioners receive a reduced rate. Moving money from super to a bank account does not hide it from Centrelink; cash is assessable and deemed.

## Concessions beyond the pension

Depending on eligibility you may access the Pensioner Concession Card, Commonwealth Seniors Health Card, rent assistance, energy supplements, and state-based concessions on rates, utilities, and transport. Each card has its own income and assets rules—some differ from Age Pension thresholds.

Aged care fees also use means-tested frameworks that reference similar but not identical definitions of income and assets. If care is on the horizon, start with [Aged care](/topics/super-retirement/manage-retirement/aged-care) and My Aged Care rather than guessing from pension paperwork alone.

First Nations readers may have access to additional community and government supports. See [First Nations services that can help](/topics/community/first-nations/first-nations-services-that-can-help) for culturally appropriate pathways.

## Applying without costly mistakes

Create or access myGov linked to Centrelink. Before lodging, gather bank statements, super summaries, property valuations, vehicle details, and evidence of income. Incomplete asset lists are a common cause of overpayments and later debts.

You can apply online, by phone, or with help from a Financial Information Service (FIS) officer—free government education, not product sales. Report changes promptly: new accounts, gifts, work, relationship changes, or moving house. "I'll tell them later" often becomes a debt letter.

Keep reference numbers from every Centrelink call. Screenshot or PDF confirmation pages where possible.

## If your payment changes

Rejections, reductions, and suspensions come with written reasons. Read them carefully—data errors (wrong account balance, missing exemption) are fixable. You have review rights if you disagree with a decision. Free financial counselling and community legal centres can help with complex cases.

Work bonus settings allow some employment income to be disregarded up to current limits—confirm thresholds on Services Australia before assuming all wages reduce your pension. Cash work "off the books" is illegal and risks penalties plus debt recovery.

For broader support contacts, see [Types of retirement income](/topics/super-retirement/income-sources/types-of-retirement-income) for how the pension fits alongside super and other sources.

## Documents that speed a claim

Create a folder with proof of identity and residence, bank statements, super and share holdings, property rates notices, vehicle registrations, and income from any work or rent. Couples need both persons' details even when only one claims. Incomplete files are the main cause of long waits.

If English is a second language, ask for an interpreter when you book. You are entitled to understand the questions being asked about your assets.

## Couples, separation, and illness

Illness or separation can change which thresholds apply and whether you are assessed as a member of a couple. Tell Centrelink promptly; silent changes create debts. If domestic violence affects your finances, ask about vulnerable-customer pathways and safer payment options.

## Renters versus homeowners

Non-homeowner asset thresholds are higher for a reason—rent continues. If you sell the home to fund aged care or downsizing, ask how long special home-sale provisions last before proceeds become ordinary assessable assets. Timing a sale around a claim can matter; verify rather than relying on a neighbour's old experience.


## What to do next

- Check your Age Pension age and current payment rates on Services Australia—not social media or old news articles.
- Use Services Australia online estimators to rough-test assets and income before you retire.
- Read [Super and the Age Pension](/topics/super-retirement/plan-retirement/super-and-the-age-pension) if you hold substantial super or an account-based pension.
- Book a free FIS appointment if means tests confuse you.
- Diary a yearly reassessment when deeming rates or thresholds update.
