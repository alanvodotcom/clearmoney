Workplace lunch-and-learns, library talks, and community groups need short, shareable retirement materials—not a product pitch deck. This page is a facilitator pack: what to open first, which stories spark discussion, and what to leave out so trust stays intact.

## Design a 45-minute session

**0–5 minutes — Frame.** Retirement planning is about cash flow, timing, and paperwork. Caps, preservation ages, and Age Pension settings change; everyone should verify figures on official sites.

**5–20 minutes — Shared checklist.** Walk the [Retirement checklist](/topics/super-retirement/retirement/retirement-checklist) on a screen. Ask participants to tick only what applies in the next 12 months—not their entire life admin.

**20–35 minutes — One case study.** Pick a single story so discussion stays deep:

- Couples and Age Pension blend → [Helen and Joe](/topics/super-retirement/retirement/case-study-helen-and-joe-retire)
- Mortgage vs super → [Bill’s mortgage decision](/topics/super-retirement/retirement/case-study-bills-mortgage-decision)
- Health shock → [Lillian’s health scare](/topics/super-retirement/retirement/case-study-lillians-health-scare)

**35–45 minutes — Tools and exit.** Show [retirement planner](/tools/retirement-planner) and [super calculator](/tools/super). End with the hang-up rule for cold-call “super checks.”

## Leave-behind sheet (one page)

Facilitators can print:

1. Link to the retirement checklist
2. Links to the two calculators above plus [super and pension age](/tools/super-pension-age) if timing confuses the room
3. Scam line: no legitimate helper needs your myGov code
4. National Debt Helpline 1800 007 007 for money stress
5. For First Nations groups: [First Nations retirement resources](/topics/super-retirement/retirement/first-nations-resources)

Do not staple third-party broker flyers that harvest mobile numbers for outbound sales.

## Running First Nations–led sessions

Lead with local services and relationships. Allow extra time for lost-super and ID questions. Use the ClearMoney [First Nations hub](/topics/community/first-nations). Avoid deficit language; focus on control, Country, and intergenerational goals. See [First Nations retirement resources](/topics/super-retirement/retirement/first-nations-resources).

## Discussion prompts that beat “any questions?”

- Which cost would hurt first if work stopped next year?
- What would you need in writing before switching a fund?
- Who can pay bills if you are in hospital for a fortnight?
- If the mortgage and super disagree, which fear is louder—and is it about maths or sleep?

## What not to do in a campaign pack

- Present case-study balances as “typical”
- Bundle the pack with a switch-your-fund incentive
- Quote contribution caps without the “verify—figures index” disclaimer
- Collect TFNs or fund logins in a group setting

## Refresh cadence

Each financial year, re-check contribution caps, SG rate, and Age Pension parameters on ATO and Services Australia pages before you reprint handouts. Update the date on your slide footer so participants know the pack is current.

## Materials you should not hand out

Skip third-party lead cards that trade a free checklist for phone numbers. Skip product-branded retirement kits that are really rollover funnels. Skip photocopies of case studies with the ClearMoney disclaimer stripped off. If you adapt discussion questions for your own workbook, keep the general-information and verify-caps line.

Facilitators sometimes over-promise calculators as truth machines. Frame them as what-if toys: change retirement age by two years, change fees by half a percent, and notice how the story moves. That habit matters more than any single projected balance.

## Keeping sessions safe for people under pressure

Open with an opt-out: anyone in active debt collection, family violence, or scam fallout can leave for [Urgent help](/urgent) without explaining themselves to the room. Put helpline numbers on the wall, not only in a slide appendix.

## Extra practical notes for Campaign resources

Rules, caps, and product features change. Before you act on anything in this guide, confirm figures with the ATO, Services Australia, your fund PDS, or a licensed adviser as relevant. ClearMoney provides general information only.

Write down the next action, the date you will complete it, and where the evidence will live (email folder, cloud drive, or paper file). If someone pressures you to skip verification, that pressure is a signal to slow down—not to hurry.

When money stress, scams, or family conflict sit behind the topic, switch from optimisation to safety: [Urgent help](/urgent), financial counselling, and official complaint pathways exist because clever tips cannot fix every situation.
