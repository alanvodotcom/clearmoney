Bill is 62, single, and has five years of mortgage left at a modest variable rate. His super could clear the loan tomorrow once he meets a condition of release—or he could keep the loan and leave super invested. This case study walks the trade-offs without declaring a winner for every household.

## Bill’s numbers (illustrative)

- Home worth about $780,000; loan balance $95,000
- Super about $410,000 in a balanced option
- Wants to reduce work to three days a week, then stop at 65
- No other large debts; emergency savings of $12,000

The emotional pull is strong: “I want the house paid off so retirement feels safe.” The financial pull is also real: money left in super may grow (and is taxed differently) compared with paying a relatively low interest rate.

## Option A — Clear the mortgage early with super

**Upside:** No compulsory repayments in retirement; lower cash-flow stress; peace of mind.

**Downside:** Permanently removes compounding on that $95,000 inside super; may reduce future Age Pension or income flexibility depending on how assets are held later; once the equity is in the house, it is harder to access without selling, borrowing, or equity release.

Bill would only do this after confirming he can access super legally (preservation age and retirement conditions). Early access scams promising “loan clearance schemes” are illegal—hang up. See [When you can access your super early](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/when-you-can-access-your-super-early).

## Option B — Keep the loan, keep super invested

**Upside:** Super stays working; he can use salary (while still working) to accelerate repayments; he keeps liquid retirement capital.

**Downside:** Rate rises hurt; he must still budget repayments when wages stop; sleep-at-night factor may be worse.

Bill stress-tested a 2 percentage point rate rise on his remaining term. If that repayment would crush a retirement budget, Option A becomes more attractive even if the “maths” on paper favour investing.

## Option C — Partial paydown

He considered withdrawing a smaller lump sum to cut the loan to a level his part-time wages could finish before 65. That preserved most of his super while shrinking the problem. Partial paydowns only help if he also shortens the term or keeps repayments high—otherwise the loan can quietly stretch.

## Downsizing as a fourth path

Selling and buying something smaller could clear the mortgage and free cash for super (including possible downsizer contributions if he qualifies). That is a lifestyle decision as much as a finance one—stamp duty, moving costs, and leaving a familiar suburb matter. See [Your home in retirement](/topics/super-retirement/plan-retirement/your-home-in-retirement) and [Downsizer super contributions](/topics/super-retirement/grow-super/downsizer-super-contributions).

Reverse mortgages and other equity release products were a last-resort conversation for Bill, not a first move—see [Reverse mortgage and home equity release](/topics/super-retirement/income-sources/reverse-mortgage-and-home-equity-release).

## What Bill chose

He kept the loan for two more working years, redirected his former dining-out budget to extra repayments, and planned a modest super lump sum at 64 only if the balance still exceeded one year of repayments. He wrote the decision down so future market noise would not yank him around.

## Questions that decide the trade-off for you

1. Is the mortgage rate meaningfully higher than what you reasonably expect from your super option after fees and tax?
2. Can you service the loan from non-super income if markets fall in the first years of retirement?
3. Do you value guaranteed lower cash obligations more than optional investment growth?
4. Would clearing the loan leave your emergency cash too thin?

## What to do next

- Write Options A–C with your real loan balance, rate, and super balance.
- Check access rules before assuming super can pay the mortgage.
- Read [Downsizing in retirement](/topics/super-retirement/manage-retirement/downsizing-in-retirement) if selling is on the table.
- Use the [retirement planner](/tools/retirement-planner) to see income with and without a mortgage repayment line.
