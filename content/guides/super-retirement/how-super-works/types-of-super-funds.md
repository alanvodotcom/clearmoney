Australian super sits in a few broad fund types. The label (industry, retail, public sector, SMSF) hints at structure and who it was built for—but you still judge any fund on fees, investments, insurance, and service.

## Industry funds

Often linked historically to unions and employer groups for a sector (health, construction, hospitality). Many are not-for-profit in structure: profits are meant to return to members rather than external shareholders. That does **not** automatically mean lowest fees or best returns every year—compare the Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) and YourSuper comparison data.

Typical strengths: scale, default MySuper products, and insurance designed for the membership. Check whether insurance definitions fit your occupation.

## Retail funds

Usually offered by banks or financial institutions and may be for-profit. They often come with broader investment menus, advice pathways, and branded apps. Fee structures can be more complex (administration + investment + advice fees).

A retail fund can be a strong fit if the net-of-fees outcome and features match you—not because the brand is familiar from your everyday banking.

## Public sector funds

Cover many government and related employees. Some older defined benefit arrangements still exist alongside accumulation accounts. Defined benefit maths is different: your retirement amount may depend on salary and years of service, not only account balance.

If you leave public employment, get written advice on what happens to defined benefits before you roll money out—irreversible choices appear here more often than in pure accumulation funds.

## Self-managed super funds (SMSFs)

An SMSF is a fund you run yourself (usually with a small number of members who are also trustees). You control investments within strict rules, and you wear the compliance load: audits, lodgements, investment strategy documents.

SMSFs suit some experienced investors with time and adequate balances; they are a poor fit if you wanted “set and forget.” Full guide: [Self-managed super fund (SMSF)](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/self-managed-super-fund-smsf).

## Corporate and legacy products

Some employers still have tailored plans or legacy products with grandparented fees or insurance. When you leave, ask what changes on exit. Stapling aims to reduce duplicate accounts when you change jobs—see [Stapled super fund](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/stapled-super-fund).

## How to compare across types

Ignore the marketing tribe. Side-by-side:

| Lens | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Fees | Total fees on *your* balance, not a brochure example |
| Investment | Option range, risk, long-term net performance |
| Insurance | Cover, cost, exclusions, what happens if you leave |
| Service | Claims help, online tools, complaints record |

Use [Choosing a super fund](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/choosing-a-super-fund) and the [fee drag](/tools/fee-drag) tool when small percentage differences compound.

## MySuper and choice products

Many funds offer a MySuper default designed for members who never pick an option, plus choice menus with wider investments. Being in an industry or retail fund does not tell you which of those you hold. Open the app and read the product name on your account—then match it to the PDS. People argue about fund categories while sitting in an expensive legacy choice product they never meant to keep.

## Ownership structure versus evidence

A not-for-profit can run a mediocre option; a retail fund can run a sharp low-fee product for a segment. Use structure as background, not as a substitute for the comparison sheet in [choosing a super fund](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/choosing-a-super-fund). If a salesperson says one category always wins, ask for the PDS pages that prove the claim for your balance size.

## What to do next
- Name your current fund type from your statement or PDS.
- If you have more than one type, list them before consolidating.
- Compare two candidates on fees and insurance—not ads.
- Only explore an SMSF after reading the SMSF guide and costing the audit.
