Claiming a super death benefit is an administrative and emotional process. Trustees must identify the right recipients, consider nominations, and often assess dependency. Timelines stretch when paperwork or family disagreement appears.

## First 48 hours: gather, don’t guess

- Death certificate (or interim evidence the fund accepts)
- The member’s fund name, member number, and latest statement if available
- Will and executor details
- Your ID and relationship evidence (marriage certificate, statutory declaration of de facto status, children’s birth certificates)
- Any binding nomination documents found in their files

Contact the fund’s bereavement team using numbers from the official website or statement—not from a random message.

## How trustees decide

If a valid binding nomination exists, payment usually follows it. Otherwise the trustee decides among dependants and/or the legal personal representative based on fund rules and law. You may be asked for financial dependency evidence (shared bills, living arrangements).

Multiple claimants can slow the outcome. Mediation beats social-media warfare; dig into facts the trustee can use.

## Insurance claims travel in parallel

If death cover sat inside super, the insurer’s claim process runs beside the trustee’s benefit decision. Medical reports and claim forms take time. See [Making a life insurance claim](/topics/insurance/life-insurance/making-a-life-insurance-claim).

## Tax and payment shape

Benefits can be paid as lump sums or, in some cases, income streams to eligible dependants. Tax depends on the recipient’s status and the benefit’s components—ask the fund for a breakdown and confirm with the ATO or a tax adviser before you spend the money.

## Practical money while you wait

Funeral costs arrive before super does. Check funeral bonds, insurance, or bank hardship options. Avoid high-cost loans pitched at grieving families. Community context: [Paying for your funeral](/topics/super-retirement/manage-retirement/paying-for-your-funeral).

## Disputes and complaints

If you disagree with a trustee decision, use the fund’s internal dispute process, then AFCA if needed. Keep every letter and deadline. Legal advice helps when estates and family law intersect.

## For executors

You may need probate before some estate-directed payments proceed. Super paid directly to a nominated dependant may never enter the estate—do not assume all balances sit in the will inventory. Cross-check with [Who gets your super if you die](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/who-gets-your-super-if-you-die).

## Communicating with co-claimants

If multiple people may claim, early transparent communication can prevent trustee stalemates. Where relationships are hostile, use solicitors sooner. Paying lawyers is painful; years of frozen benefits are worse.

## Working with the legal personal representative

If you are not the executor but believe you are a dependant, say so early and supply evidence. Executors and dependants sometimes clash; trustees must follow the deed and law. Keep communication factual and dated.

## Overseas beneficiaries

Claimants living overseas face extra identity and banking checks. Start early, use certified documents as the fund specifies, and ask about tax withholding that may apply to non-dependants. Delays multiply across borders.

## Fresh-numbers habit for claiming a super death benefit

Numbers from last year’s seminar may already be wrong. Caps, deeming rates, and insurance definitions move. Re-check primary sources the week you act. If a salesperson becomes irritated when you pause to verify, that irritation reveals their incentives—not your intelligence.

## Continuity checklist for Claiming a super death benefit

List the people, passwords managers, fund member numbers, and Centrelink CRNs that keep this topic running when you are travelling or unwell. Tape the list inside a secure place at home. Continuity is part of money skill, not paranoia.

## What to do next
- Notify each fund in writing and ask for their bereavement checklist.
- Lodge insurance claim forms early if cover existed.
- Store claim reference numbers beside the will documents.
- Seek tax advice before distributing or investing a large benefit.
