Stapling links you to an existing super account so a new employer does not automatically open yet another fund. The aim is fewer duplicate accounts, fewer fee stacks, and less lost super—provided your stapled fund is actually a fund you want.

## What stapling does in practice

When you start a job, your employer asks the ATO which fund is stapled to you. If one exists, SG contributions generally go there unless you validly choose a different fund under the rules that apply to that workplace.

You are not glued forever. You can still request a different fund; stapling mainly changes the **default** path that used to create new accounts on every hire.

## Why duplicate accounts were a problem

Short contracts and casual work left people with many low balances. Each balance paid administration fees and sometimes insurance. Small accounts also went “lost” when addresses aged out. Stapling reduces that factory of fragments—but it does not clean up accounts you already have. You may still need [consolidating super funds](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/consolidating-super-funds) or [find lost super](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/find-lost-super).

## Check what you are stapled to

Log in to myGov / ATO super information and your fund app. Confirm:

- Fund name and member number
- Whether insurance is still appropriate
- Whether fees on that balance are reasonable
- That your TFN and contact details are current

If your stapled fund is high-fee or has cover you hate, plan a deliberate switch—do not assume stapling equals endorsement. Use [Choosing a super fund](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/choosing-a-super-fund).

## New job checklist

1. Ask payroll which fund they will pay if you do nothing
2. Decide: keep stapled fund or lodge a choice of fund form
3. After the first payslip cycle, confirm SG hit the right account (Payday Super timing rules are tightening when employers must pay—see [What is payday super?](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/what-is-payday-super))
4. Update your résumé of funds so old accounts are not forgotten

## When stapling does not solve everything

- Defined benefit or legacy public sector schemes with special rules
- Employer funds that only accept current staff for some features
- SMSFs (different administration world)
- People with no existing account who still receive a default MySuper product

If you are starting work for the first time, you may still land in a default fund—review it within the first months.

## Stapling vs “just leave it”

Staying stapled to a mediocre product for twenty years is expensive silence. Review every two years or at each job change: fees, insurance, investment option. Stapling stops accidental sprawl; it does not replace judgement.

## Onboarding scripts that confuse new starters

Some HR packs still read as if everyone must join the employer historic default. Politely ask for the Superannuation Standard Choice form or digital equivalent and nominate your stapled or preferred fund. If payroll says their software only accepts Fund X, escalate—software limitations are not always legal requirements.

Casual employees and labour-hire arrangements can create messy stapling trails when ABNs and employing entities differ. Keep every payslip. When contributions appear under an unexpected employer name, match the ABN to your contract before assuming fraud.

## Review cadence after stapling

Stapling reduces clutter; it does not retire your brain. Every two job changes—or every three years, whichever comes first—re-run fees and insurance. A fund that was fine at a small balance may look expensive later, or flat-dollar admin fees may hurt early balances more.

## Extra practical notes for Stapled super fund

Rules, caps, and product features change. Before you act on anything in this guide, confirm figures with the ATO, Services Australia, your fund PDS, or a licensed adviser as relevant. ClearMoney provides general information only.

Write down the next action, the date you will complete it, and where the evidence will live (email folder, cloud drive, or paper file). If someone pressures you to skip verification, that pressure is a signal to slow down—not to hurry.

When money stress, scams, or family conflict sit behind the topic, switch from optimisation to safety: [Urgent help](/urgent), financial counselling, and official complaint pathways exist because clever tips cannot fix every situation.

## What to do next
- Look up your stapled fund in myGov this week.
- After your next job change, verify the first SG deposit destination.
- If the stapled fund fails a fee/insurance smell test, shortlist a replacement.
- Hunt down any non-stapled leftovers and decide whether to consolidate carefully.
