If someone rings offering a free super “health check,” performance review, or lost-super recovery, assume sales until proven otherwise. Legitimate fund staff you already bank with do not need to scare you into an immediate switch—and they will not ask for one-time codes.

## Lines you will hear

- “We work with ASIC / your fund / the ATO” (name-dropping without proof)
- “Your fees are shocking—we can fix it today”
- “You’ve got lost super; pay us a percentage to unlock it”
- “Stay on the line while we install support software”
- “Confirm your myGov SMS code so we can verify you”

Each is a reason to hang up. You can search lost super free via myGov. Fee comparisons are free via official tools and PDSs.

## What a real review looks like

You initiate contact through your fund’s published phone number or secure app chat. You download statements yourself. If you use an adviser, you verify they are on the [Financial advisers register](/topics/investing-planning/advice/financial-advisers-register) and you receive a written scope and fee.

A stranger who called you is not that process.

## Protect the channels

- Do not install remote-access apps at a caller’s request
- Do not read out SMS codes
- Do not photograph your driver’s licence for a cold caller
- Prefer to call back on a number from your statement after ending the call

If you already shared details, contact your fund on an official number, change passwords, and report to Scamwatch / ReportCyber. See [Superannuation scams](/topics/scams-safety/financial-scams/superannuation-scams) and [What to do if you’ve been scammed](/topics/scams-safety/check-report/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed).

## Family and older relatives

Retirees are frequent targets. Agree a household rule: no super decisions on inbound calls. Put fund numbers on the fridge. Discuss [Protect your money in retirement](/topics/super-retirement/manage-retirement/protect-your-money-in-retirement).

## If you still want to switch funds

Run your own comparison using [Choosing a super fund](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/choosing-a-super-fund) and [Switching super funds](/topics/super-retirement/grow-super/switching-super-funds). Sleep on it. Real fee savings will still be there tomorrow.

## Verification steps unique to Protect your super from pushy sales calls

Confirm live contribution caps, Age Pension thresholds, preservation details, and product fees on official ATO, Services Australia, or fund PDS pages the week you act. ClearMoney offers general information only—not personal advice. Write one next action with a date and store PDFs of confirmations where you can find them in six months.

If the dollar amount is large, sleep on the decision and consider licensed advice. Cold callers who demand one-time codes or remote access are not helpers. Hang up and use bookmarks you typed yourself.

## Filing habits for protect your super from pushy sales calls

Keep statements, insurance schedules, advice fee consents, and solicitor letters in a dated folder. Share access rules with a trusted person without pasting every password into a group chat. Organised evidence shortens disputes, estate claims, and Centrelink conversations.

Revisit after job changes, relationship changes, or health shocks. A short annual review beats a perfect plan nobody opens.

## Fresh-numbers habit for protect your super from pushy sales calls

Numbers from last year’s seminar may already be wrong. Caps, deeming rates, and insurance definitions move. Re-check primary sources the week you act. If a salesperson becomes irritated when you pause to verify, that irritation reveals their incentives—not your intelligence.

## Continuity checklist for Protect your super from pushy sales calls

List the people, passwords managers, fund member numbers, and Centrelink CRNs that keep this topic running when you are travelling or unwell. Tape the list inside a secure place at home. Continuity is part of money skill, not paranoia.

## What to do next
- Practice a one-sentence hang-up: “I don’t take super calls—send nothing.”
- Enable fund app alerts for details changes.
- Show an older relative how to find the official fund number on a statement.
- Report aggressive callers so patterns get visibility.
