Contributions are how super grows beyond investment returns. The law splits them into buckets with different tax treatments and annual caps. Dollar caps index—**verify this year’s figures with the ATO** before you salary-sacrifice a large sum.

::: tip
Check this year’s contribution caps on the ATO site you typed yourself—marketing PDFs go stale fast.
:::

:::

## Concessional contributions

These include:

- Employer Superannuation Guarantee
- Salary sacrifice
- Personal contributions you successfully claim as a tax deduction

They count toward the concessional cap. Inside the fund they are generally taxed at 15% on entry (Division 293 may add extra tax for higher earners—check thresholds).

Because SG uses part of the cap, high SG on a large salary leaves less room for sacrifice. Add projected SG + sacrifice on paper first.

## Non-concessional contributions

Usually after-tax money you add voluntarily. They generally are not taxed again on entry but have a separate cap. Bring-forward rules can allow up to multiple years’ caps in one go if you are eligible and your total super balance is under the relevant threshold on 30 June of the prior year—details move with indexation.

If your total super balance is too high, your non-concessional cap can be nil.

## Other labelled contributions

- **Downsizer** — from eligible home sale proceeds; separate rules and age eligibility—see [Downsizer super contributions](/topics/super-retirement/grow-super/downsizer-super-contributions)
- **Spouse contributions** — may attract a tax offset within limits
- **Government co-contribution** — for eligible low/middle income earners making non-concessional contributions
- **Carry-forward concessional** — unused cap amounts from prior years may be available if balance tests are met

Each has eligibility traps. Read ATO pages for the year you contribute.

## Work test and age settings

Older Australians face evolving rules about when personal deductible contributions or certain contributions are allowed. Confirm work-test / age settings for your birthday cohort rather than relying on a 2019 blog post.

## A clean salary-sacrifice setup

1. Confirm remaining concessional cap after SG
2. Complete employer forms to start on a pay-cycle boundary
3. Check the first fund credit matches the payslip
4. Reassess each July when caps index and salaries change

Model impact with the [super calculator](/tools/super). Self-employed readers should also see [Super for self-employed people](/topics/super-retirement/grow-super/super-for-self-employed-people).

## Notices, deadlines, and June chaos

Personal deductible contributions fail when the notice of intent is late or never acknowledged. Build a May checklist: estimate room, transfer, lodge notice, wait for acknowledgement, then prepare the tax return. Funds get slammed in June—start earlier.

## Interaction with HECS and cash flow

Salary sacrifice lowers take-home pay. If you also repay HECS-HELP or support a household on a tight margin, model three months of payslips before you lock a percentage. Contribution strategies that create grocery stress will be abandoned, and abandoned automations help nobody.

## Fresh-numbers habit for super contributions

Numbers from last year’s seminar may already be wrong. Caps, deeming rates, and insurance definitions move. Re-check primary sources the week you act. If a salesperson becomes irritated when you pause to verify, that irritation reveals their incentives—not your intelligence.

## Continuity checklist for Super contributions

List the people, passwords managers, fund member numbers, and Centrelink CRNs that keep this topic running when you are travelling or unwell. Tape the list inside a secure place at home. Continuity is part of money skill, not paranoia.

## Government co-contribution reality checks

Low-income co-contribution and similar incentives only help if you meet eligibility tests and lodge correctly. Never assume a tip from a mate still applies this year—confirm on the ATO site before you rely on free matching money.

## What to do next
- Look up the current concessional and non-concessional caps on the ATO site.
- Calculate SG’s bite of your concessional room before you sacrifice.
- Automate a modest ongoing contribution rather than one stressful June lump sum—see [Get your super working over time](/topics/super-retirement/grow-super/get-your-super-working-over-time).
- Keep contribution notices if you claim deductions.
