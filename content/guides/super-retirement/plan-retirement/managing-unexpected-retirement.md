Not every retirement is chosen. Redundancy, caring duties, injury, or chronic illness can end paid work years earlier than the spreadsheet assumed. The goal shifts from optimisation to stabilising cash flow without torching the long-term plan—or falling for scams that promise early super access.

If essentials are at risk this week, use [Urgent help](/urgent) and [urgent help with money](/topics/loans-credit-debt/managing-debt/urgent-help-with-money) before you make irreversible super decisions.

## The first fortnight: stabilise income and bills

Map severance, leave payouts, and final payslips line by line. Register for any eligible income support quickly—delays cost money. Cut non-essential outgoings before you touch super. Talk to lenders about hardship if mortgage or rent is shaky. Check income protection or TPD through super and any work cover while deadlines are fresh.

Employment admin—final pay, references, insurance continuity—lives in [losing your job](/topics/banking-budgeting/work-tax/losing-your-job). Treat that guide and this one as parallel tracks: paperwork on one side, cash flow on the other.

## Super access: legal pathways versus harmful shortcuts

You may meet a condition of release earlier than planned—for example permanent incapacity—or you may not. Illegal early-access schemes prey on newly redundant workers: they ask for identity documents, charge fees, and leave you with tax debts and empty accounts. Refuse them.

See [When you can access your super early](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/when-you-can-access-your-super-early) and [Getting your super](/topics/super-retirement/how-super-works/getting-your-super) for lawful options. If you can leave super invested while bridging with other income, compounding continues. Cashing out large sums to “sit on” often triggers tax, loses insurance, and shrinks what is left for true retirement.

## Rebuild a leaner twelve-month plan

Recast spending to a survival budget first, then a sustainable one. Delay large discretionary hits—new car, major renovations, generous gifts. Re-run the [retirement planner](/tools/retirement-planner) with the new stop-work date and lower income assumptions.

Part-time or consulting work, even one day a week, can change the maths and the social rhythm. Update your skills listing and network before isolation sets in; the next role may not look like the last one.

## Health-led exits need documentation early

Obtain clear medical documentation for insurance and super claims. Do not let policy deadlines lapse while you focus on treatment. Pair money steps with care planning—transport, home help, and how bills get paid if energy is low.

If cognitive or physical decline is involved, think about decision-making documents before capacity becomes contested—see [Memory loss, dementia and your money](/topics/super-retirement/manage-retirement/memory-loss-dementia-and-your-money) when relevant.

## Emotional spending traps after shock

Shock shopping, “I deserve this trip,” or lending lump-sum payouts to family can erase a bridge buffer in weeks. Park severance in a separate account overnight before deciding anything irreversible. A cooling-off rule—no gifts or investments over a set amount for thirty days—sounds rigid and often saves regret.

## When stability returns, reconnect to the long plan

Once income support, hardship arrangements, and essential bills are under control, reopen [make a retirement plan](/topics/super-retirement/plan-retirement/make-a-retirement-plan) with the new facts: earlier finish date, possibly lower super balance, maybe different housing needs. The document should reflect reality, not the job you lost.

Unexpected retirement is a chapter, not the whole book. People who recover financially often do so by protecting super until a lawful condition applies, keeping skills active, and revising spending without shame.

## Support services worth knowing

Financial counsellors offer free, confidential help prioritising debts and Centrelink paperwork—distinct from advisers who sell products. If redundancy came with a small package, a counsellor can help you sequence bill payments before any irreversible super withdrawal. They cannot tell you which super product to buy, but they can stop a bad week from becoming a bad decade.

## What to do next

- Stabilise income support and hardship requests before any product or super withdrawal decision.
- Confirm whether any legal super access condition actually applies to your situation.
- Rewrite a 12-month lean cash-flow plan on one page and share it with anyone who shares the bills.
- Park severance in a separate account and wait one week before major commitments.
- When stable, rebuild your plan using [Make a retirement plan](/topics/super-retirement/plan-retirement/make-a-retirement-plan).
