Life insurance pays a lump sum if you die (and sometimes if you are diagnosed with a terminal illness). The point is not “more cover is always better”—it is matching a sum insured to the debts and dependants who would otherwise face a shortfall.

::: tip
Subtract cover you already hold in super before you buy more retail life insurance.
:::

::: tool relatedTools[0]
:::

## Who actually needs a lump-sum payout?

Ask who would struggle financially if your income and unpaid labour stopped tomorrow. Common triggers for needing cover include:

- a partner or children who rely on your wages
- a mortgage or other debts you want cleared so the household can stay put
- funeral and estate costs you do not want to push onto relatives

If nobody depends on your income and you have no debts that would fall to others, a large retail life policy may be optional. Many people still hold a modest amount of default cover inside [super](/topics/insurance/life-insurance/insurance-through-super)—worth checking before you buy more.

Sole traders and contractors often need more deliberate cover because sick leave and employer-funded defaults are thin. Dual-income couples should size cover for each earner, not only the higher salary.

## How to size the sum insured

Work from cash needs, not from a rule of thumb you saw online. A simple stack:

1. Debts you want paid off (mortgage balance, personal loans, credit cards).
2. Years of income replacement for dependants (often several years of living costs, not every year until retirement).
3. One-off costs (funeral, probate, childcare transition, education buffer if that matters to you).
4. Subtract savings, existing life cover (including in super), and any assets you would actually sell.

Use the [insurance needs calculator](/tools/insurance-needs) to turn those figures into a working total. Round up for inflation and underestimation—then test whether the premium still fits your budget. A sum insured you cannot sustain is useless when you cancel later.

Stepped premiums usually start cheaper and rise with age. Level premiums cost more early and are more stable later. Neither is automatically “better”; what matters is whether you will still be paying when cover is most needed.

## Exclusions and fine print that shrink a claim

Policy wording decides outcomes more than the marketing name. Watch for:

- **Suicide exclusion periods** — often the first 13 months on new cover.
- **Non-disclosure** — incomplete health or smoking answers can void or reduce a claim years later.
- **Hazardous activities and occupations** — loadings or exclusions for certain jobs and sports.
- **Terminal illness definitions** — how many months’ life expectancy the insurer requires before an early payout.
- **Ownership and beneficiaries** — whether the policy sits in your name, a trust, or super, and who can claim.

Read the product disclosure statement for waiting periods on any bundled benefits, and confirm what happens if you stop work or move overseas. If you already have cover through super, compare definitions side by side before stacking retail policies—you may be paying twice for overlapping protection.

## Keeping cover aligned as life changes

Review the sum insured when you buy a home, have a child, separate, pay down debt, or change jobs. A mortgage that has halved may mean less cover is enough; a new dependant may mean the opposite. Update nominations so the right people can be paid without needless delay.

If premium rises make you want to cancel, ask about reducing the sum insured or switching structures before you drop protection entirely—especially if health changes would make new cover hard to get.

## What to do next

- List debts, dependants, and existing cover (super and retail), then run [Insurance needs](/tools/insurance-needs).
- Compare your default [insurance through super](/topics/insurance/life-insurance/insurance-through-super) before buying a new retail policy.
- Read exclusions, suicide clauses, and terminal-illness rules in the PDS—not only the premium quote.
- Decide ownership and beneficiaries in writing, and diary an annual review date.
- If income replacement matters more than a death benefit, also read [income protection](/topics/insurance/life-insurance/income-protection-insurance) and [TPD](/topics/insurance/life-insurance/total-and-permanent-disability-tpd-insurance).
