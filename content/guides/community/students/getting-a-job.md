Your first job is where money systems stop being theoretical. Tax file numbers, payslips, and super contributions appear quickly—and mistakes are easier to fix in week one than in June.

## Before your first shift

Have ready:

- **Tax file number (TFN)** — apply via the ATO if you do not have one; working without a TFN can mean tax withheld at a higher rate
- **Bank account in your name** — so wages are not paid into someone else’s account
- **Super choice form** if your employer asks—otherwise you may be stapled to an existing fund or placed in the employer default
- **Right-to-work documents** your employer legally needs to sight

Read the Fair Work Information Statement your employer should give you. Note your casual or permanent status, loading, and penalty rates if they apply. Screenshot or keep the roster rules so underpayment is easier to spot later.

## Reading the payslip without panic

Every payslip should show who paid you, the period, hours or salary, gross pay, tax withheld, net pay, and super contributions for the period (and year-to-date figures are common).

Check:

- Hours match what you worked (including penalties and loadings)
- Tax withheld looks plausible for your income—use the [income tax tool](/tools/income-tax) for a rough sense, and [first payslip](/topics/banking-budgeting/work-tax/first-payslip) for a line-by-line walkthrough
- Super is listed—absence after the relevant period is a red flag to raise early

If something is wrong, ask payroll in writing with the payslip attached. Keep copies. Underpayments compound quietly across a semester.

## Tax file number, tax, and refunds

Giving your TFN to your employer is normal. Giving your TFN to random websites or “job offer” emails is not—treat those as scam risks.

You may get a refund at tax time if too much tax was withheld, or a bill if too little was. Keep:

- Payslips
- Deduction records only if they are legitimate work expenses (students often overestimate what is claimable)
- HECS/HELP details if relevant to your notice of assessment later

Casual work across multiple employers can mean tax does not even out perfectly during the year—another reason to keep records.

## Super from day one

Super is part of your pay, paid by the employer into a fund (subject to eligibility rules that have expanded over time—verify current thresholds with the ATO if needed). Small balances from short jobs add up and can be lost track of.

After a few pays:

- Log in to the fund or use myGov/ATO to confirm contributions arrived
- Avoid opening duplicate funds every time you change café jobs if stapling already links you
- Do not entertain cold calls offering a “free super check”

More on reading contributions sits in [income tax basics](/topics/banking-budgeting/work-tax/income-tax) and workplace guides linked from your payslip article.

## Multiple casual jobs

Two casual roles can mean two tax withholdings that do not even out until you lodge. Track both payslips. Watch total hours for fatigue and course risk. Tell each employer your availability honestly so roster clashes do not cost you shifts or grades.

Join or note your union or student workplace rights contacts early. Knowing who to call about underpayment is part of the first-job toolkit.

## What to do next

- Apply for a TFN if you need one, and open a bank account in your own name before payday.
- On first pay, tick off hours, tax, net, and super against your roster.
- Save PDFs of payslips in a folder named by employer and year.
- Confirm your super fund login and that money is landing.
- If study and work collide, revisit your [studying](/topics/community/students/studying) cash-flow plan with the new take-home numbers.
