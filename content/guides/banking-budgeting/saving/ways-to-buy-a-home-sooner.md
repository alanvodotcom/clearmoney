Buying sooner is rarely one clever trick. It is usually a mix of growing the deposit faster, shrinking purchase costs, and choosing borrowing settings that do not create a larger problem later. Every shortcut has a trade-off—name it before you take it.

## The three levers: deposit, price, and costs

Time-to-buy shrinks when any of these move:

1. **More cash for the deposit** — higher transfers, windfalls, shared saving, or eligible assistance.
2. **Lower purchase price** — different suburb, property type, or accepting a fixer within your skill and budget.
3. **Lower cash needed at settlement** — concessions, fee choices, and avoiding last-minute surprises.

Changing only the loan structure (interest-only periods, longer terms, thinner deposits with LMI) can bring a settlement date forward while raising lifetime cost or risk. Use the [Mortgage calculator](/tools/mortgage) to compare repayment shapes before you treat a product feature as a “hack.”

If you do not yet have a deposit plan, start with [save for a house deposit](/topics/banking-budgeting/saving/save-for-a-house-deposit). This page assumes you already know your rough target and want to compress the wait.

## Growing the deposit faster

Raise the automated transfer whenever income rises—overtime, a new job, a cancelled subscription stack from [simple ways to save money](/topics/banking-budgeting/saving/simple-ways-to-save-money). Redirect tax refunds and bonuses by default into the deposit account.

Consider a temporary “sprint”: six months of stricter discretionary spending with a written end date, so the squeeze does not become permanent resentment. Sell unused goods once; do not rely on endless garage sales as a strategy.

Shared goals need shared numbers. Couples who combine surplus into one nicknamed account often move faster than two people casually “trying.” [Savings goals](/tools/savings-goals) helps you re-forecast when the fortnightly amount jumps.

Family gifts or loans can accelerate a purchase. Document terms. A gift that is actually a loan with unspoken repayment expectations creates conflict at the worst time. Lenders will ask for paperwork either way.

## Cutting purchase costs that eat equity

Stamp duty, conveyancing, inspections, and moving rarely appear on Instagram home tours—and they delay settlement as surely as a thin deposit. Check whether you may qualify for first-home or regional concessions in your state or territory; rules change, so verify on official sites before you budget zero.

Shop conveyancers and building inspectors on scope, not only price. A cheap inspection that misses structural issues is not a saving. Budget a contingency for repairs discovered late.

Lenders mortgage insurance (LMI) can let you buy with a smaller deposit. It protects the lender, not you, and it adds cost—sometimes capitalised into the loan. Compare the LMI path against waiting for a larger deposit using real repayment figures, not vibes.

## Borrowing trade-offs that shrink the wait

A longer loan term lowers repayments and can improve serviceability, which may help you borrow enough to buy sooner—but you pay more interest over the life of the loan unless you overpay later. Interest-only periods reduce early repayments and can be useful in narrow cases; they also delay principal reduction.

Borrowing the absolute maximum a bank offers is how people become house-stressed. Serviceability at today’s rate is not the same as comfort when rates, children, or job changes arrive. Leave headroom on purpose.

Guarantors (for example parents offering security) can reduce deposit pressure. They also put someone else’s home or savings at risk if you default. Treat that as a family financial decision, not a paperwork formality.

## Schemes and boosts—read before you rely

First-home buyer grants, guarantees, and shared-equity style schemes can shorten the cash runway. Eligibility usually hinges on citizenship or residency, property price caps, prior ownership, and whether the home will be your residence. Conditions and availability change—confirm on government sources and with your lender or adviser, not from a social media summary.

Never spend money you have not been approved for. Scheme timing and lender assessment still have to align with your settlement date.

Faster is not always better. Buying a property you cannot maintain, in a location that blocks work, or without any [emergency fund](/topics/banking-budgeting/saving/save-for-an-emergency-fund), can cost more than another year of renting while you prepare.

## What to do next

- List which lever you will pull first: higher deposit transfers, lower price band, or lower settlement costs.
- Re-run [Savings goals](/tools/savings-goals) with a stretched transfer amount and a nearer date—see if the gap closes.
- Model LMI vs waiting, and different loan terms, in [Mortgage calculator](/tools/mortgage).
- Check official first-home or concession pages for your state or territory and note price caps.
- Keep a minimum cash buffer so “sooner” does not mean “one bill away from arrears.”
