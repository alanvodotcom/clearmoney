Australian income tax is progressive: higher slices of your taxable income are taxed at higher rates. Most employees never write a cheque to the ATO mid-year because employers withhold tax from each pay. Understanding the labels—taxable income, withholding, Medicare levy—helps you read a payslip and plan for lodgement without panic.

## What “taxable income” means

**Taxable income** is not your bank balance and not always the same as your salary package. In broad terms it is assessable income minus deductions the tax rules allow. Assessable income often includes salary and wages, interest, dividends, some government payments, and business profit if you are self-employed.

Deductions are expenses you incurred to earn that income, within ATO rules—not every receipt from the year. Work-related expenses, donations to deductible gifts, and the cost of managing tax affairs are common categories for employees. Rules change; keep records and check current guidance before you claim.

Your **taxable income** is what the tax rates and the Medicare levy are applied to when you lodge. Withholding during the year is an estimate against that eventual bill.

## How tax comes out of your pay

When you start a job you complete a tax file number (TFN) declaration. That tells your employer how to withhold: resident status, whether you claim the tax-free threshold, HECS-HELP, and similar flags. Get those wrong and you can under-withhold (owe at lodgement) or over-withhold (a larger refund, but tighter cash flow all year).

Each payslip usually shows:

- **Gross** — pay before tax and most deductions
- **PAYG withholding** — tax held for the ATO
- **Net** — what lands in your account after withholding and other deductions

Withholding tables are designed so that, for a single steady job claiming the threshold, you are roughly on track. A second job, a big bonus, salary packaging, or irregular overtime can throw the estimate off. That is normal—it is why annual lodgement exists.

Use the [Income Tax](/tools/income-tax) calculator to turn a gross figure into an approximate tax and take-home estimate. Treat the result as a planning number, not an official assessment.

## The Medicare levy in plain language

Most Australian residents also pay a **Medicare levy**, usually calculated as a percentage of taxable income. It helps fund Medicare. It is separate from the income tax rates, but it appears on the same assessment when you lodge.

Low-income earners may pay a reduced levy or none, depending on thresholds that change over time. Some people also face a **Medicare levy surcharge** if they earn above certain levels and do not hold qualifying private hospital cover. The surcharge is not the same as the levy; it is an extra amount designed to encourage hospital cover at higher incomes.

If a colleague says “tax is X percent,” ask whether they mean the marginal income-tax rate only, or tax plus Medicare. Mixing those labels causes most dinner-table arguments about “what bracket I’m in.”

## Marginal rates vs your average rate

Australia uses **marginal** rates: only the income in each bracket is taxed at that bracket’s rate. Crossing into a higher bracket does not re-tax your whole income at the higher rate.

Your **average** tax rate (total tax ÷ taxable income) is usually lower than your top marginal rate. When people say a pay rise “isn’t worth it,” they sometimes forget that only the extra dollars are taxed at the higher marginal rate—and that Medicare and HELP repayments can also change with income.

## Mistakes that create a surprise bill

- Claiming the tax-free threshold on two jobs at once
- Ignoring interest, shares, or crypto when you lodge
- Assuming a refund is guaranteed because “I always get one”
- Forgetting that private health and family circumstances can change Medicare outcomes

If your situation is simple—one employer, little other income—you may lodge yourself through myTax. If you have a side business, complex investments, or a prior ATO debt, read [Choosing an accountant](/topics/banking-budgeting/work-tax/choosing-an-accountant) before tax time rushes you.

## What to do next

- Sketch this year’s expected income sources (jobs, interest, side gigs) on one page.
- Check your latest [payslip](/topics/banking-budgeting/work-tax/first-payslip) for withholding and TFN flags that look wrong.
- Run a rough estimate with [Income Tax](/tools/income-tax), then verify rates and thresholds on official ATO pages before you rely on the number.
- Put [lodging a tax return](/topics/banking-budgeting/work-tax/lodging-a-tax-return) on your calendar with a records checklist, not just a deadline date.
