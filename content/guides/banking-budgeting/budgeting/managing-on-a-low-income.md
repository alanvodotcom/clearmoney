Living on a tight income is not a budgeting failure—it is a maths problem with limited room. Prioritise what keeps you housed, fed, and safe, then use free support before high-cost credit.

## Essentials first, everything else later

When money is short, order payments by consequence:

1. **Shelter** — rent or mortgage, then essential utilities that keep the home liveable.
2. **Food and medicine** — groceries and prescriptions before optional shopping.
3. **Work and care transport** — getting to paid work, school, or caring duties.
4. **Minimum debt repayments** — protect your options; contact lenders early if you cannot pay.
5. **Everything else** — pause until the above are covered.

Write this order on paper or in your phone. When a “must have” pitch arrives, compare it to the list—not to the salesperson’s urgency. Stretch tactics for rising prices sit in [Cost of living help](/topics/banking-budgeting/budgeting/cost-of-living-help).

Use the [Simple Money Manager](/tools/simple-money-manager) to see the week ahead in one view. A light weekly tool beats a complex annual spreadsheet you never open. If step 1–3 already exceed income, you need support and negotiation—not a stricter colouring-in budget. Start at [/urgent](/urgent).

## Free and low-cost support in Australia

Ask early—waiting until disconnection notices arrive narrows your options:

- **Financial counsellors** — free, independent help with budgets, debts, and creditor talks. Start via the National Debt Helpline on 1800 007 007.
- **Centrelink and concessions** — check payments, supplements, and Health Care Card eligibility if your situation changed.
- **Energy and water hardship** — most retailers must offer payment plans and hardship programs; ask for them by name.
- **Emergency relief** — food, vouchers, or bill help through local community organisations (Ask Izzy and council directories are common starting points).
- **Legal help** — community legal centres for tenancy, fines, and consumer issues.
- **Family violence and financial abuse** — if someone controls money or restricts essentials, that can be abuse. Help is available through 1800RESPECT (1800 737 732) and [/urgent](/urgent).

None of this is charity theatre—it is how the system is meant to work when income is low. Keep reference numbers from every call and follow up in writing when a payment plan is agreed.

## Protecting cash flow week by week

Low income often fails on *timing*, not only totals:

- Align bill due dates with payday where providers allow it.
- Keep a tiny buffer if you can—even $20–$50—so a late bus fare does not bounce a direct debit.
- Prefer fortnightly grocery shops with a fixed list over daily top-ups that inflate spend.
- Avoid “buy now, pay later” for groceries and bills; the repayments stack on top of an already tight week.
- Cancel paid extras before cutting medical needs or children’s essentials.

If hours bounce around, borrow methods from [Managing on a casual income](/topics/banking-budgeting/budgeting/managing-on-a-casual-income)—budget from a quiet week, bank surplus in good weeks.

Track for one fortnight so the picture is honest: [Track your spending](/topics/banking-budgeting/budgeting/track-your-spending). Then lock essentials into a simple plan with [How to do a budget](/topics/banking-budgeting/budgeting/how-to-do-a-budget).

## When debt or hardship enters the picture

High-interest loans, payday products, and unpaid fines make a low income harder. Prefer:

- Talking to existing creditors about hardship before taking new debt.
- Free financial counselling over paid “debt agreement” ads that charge fees.
- Checking whether a fine can be contested, paid by instalments, or converted to community options where available.

Billers and landlords usually have more options before default listings and disconnection. A short honest call with a payment-date proposal is a skill, not a failure. If you feel unsafe about money in a relationship, prioritise personal safety and sole access to essential income before joint arrangements.

## What to do next

- List essentials in priority order and fund them before flexible spend.
- Map the coming week in the [Simple Money Manager](/tools/simple-money-manager).
- Call 1800 007 007 or a local financial counsellor if debts or bills are overwhelming.
- Use [Cost of living help](/topics/banking-budgeting/budgeting/cost-of-living-help) for concessions, rebates, and stretch tactics.
