A personal loan is a fixed amount borrowed for a set term, repaid on a schedule. It can be useful for a planned expense—or expensive if the weekly figure hides a long, costly term.

## What a personal loan actually is

Most personal loans in Australia are either:

- **Unsecured** — no asset pledged; rates are usually higher because the lender takes more risk.
- **Secured** — something (often a car) backs the loan; rates can be lower, but you risk losing the security if you default.

You repay principal plus interest (and any fees) over months or years. Missed payments can damage your credit file and may lead to debt collection. Unlike a credit card, you typically receive one lump sum up front and then grind the balance down—there is usually no “revolving” credit left to spend again.

Before you apply, write the purpose in one sentence. “Consolidate three cards” or “replace a failed hot-water system” is clearer than “need money.” Vague purposes make it harder to judge whether waiting, saving, or a different product would cost less.

## When borrowing beats waiting

Borrowing can make sense when:

- the expense is **necessary and time-sensitive** (essential repairs, medical costs not covered elsewhere)
- the **total cost of the loan** is less than the cost of delaying (for example, further damage to a home system)
- you can repay from **steady income** without skipping rent, food, or utilities
- you have compared the loan against **cheaper options**—family help with clear terms, a no-interest community loan for essentials, or paying from savings

It usually does **not** make sense for holidays, gadgets, or lifestyle upgrades you cannot afford after a short cooling-off period. If you are already juggling multiple debts, a new personal loan can deepen the hole—see [get debt under control](/topics/loans-credit-debt/managing-debt/get-debt-under-control) and [urgent help](/urgent) if cash for essentials is short. Call the National Debt Helpline on **1800 007 007** for free guidance.

## What to compare before you sign

Ignore the weekly repayment as your only number. Line up offers on the same loan amount and term, and compare:

- **Interest rate** and whether it is fixed or variable
- **Comparison rate** (where provided)—it folds in many fees so products are easier to rank
- **Establishment and monthly fees**
- **Early repayment rules**—can you pay out early without a large break cost?
- **Total amount repayable** over the full term
- **Insurance add-ons**—consumer credit insurance is often expensive relative to cover; decline unless you have checked alternatives

Use the [personal loan calculator](/tools/personal-loan) to see how term length changes total interest. Then put two or three real quotes into the [loan comparison](/tools/loan-comparison) tool so the cheapest-looking weekly figure does not win by default.

Lenders assess serviceability: income, existing debts, living costs, and your credit history. Applying everywhere at once can leave multiple enquiries on your file. Shortlist first, then apply.

## Secured vs unsecured in practice

Secured loans can look cheaper on the rate. Price the risk too: if the security is a car you need for work, default is not only a credit problem—it can cut your income. Unsecured loans cost more in interest but leave your assets out of the contract (subject to normal debt recovery rules).

If a broker or lender pushes you to borrow more than you asked for “while you’re approved,” refuse. Extra principal means extra interest for years. Take contracts home overnight if anyone rushes you.

## What to do next

- Write the purpose, amount, and the maximum monthly repayment your budget can stress-test.
- Run scenarios in the [personal loan](/tools/personal-loan) and [loan comparison](/tools/loan-comparison) tools before you apply.
- Compare at least two lenders on total repayable, not weekly payments alone.
- If debt stress is already present, contact the National Debt Helpline on **1800 007 007** or start at [/urgent](/urgent) before taking on more credit.
