Your credit report is a history file lenders use when you apply for loans, cards, and sometimes utilities or rentals. Your score is a summary number derived from that history—useful, but not a moral rating.

## What sits on your credit report

Reports commonly include:

- personal identification details and addresses
- open and closed credit accounts
- repayment history information on eligible accounts
- defaults and serious credit infringements (where recorded)
- credit enquiries from applications
- some court judgments or insolvency information
- whether you have corrected or disputed information

Different credit reporting bodies may hold slightly different snapshots. A clean report at one bureau does not guarantee identical content at another—check the main ones used in Australia.

Scores vary by model and supplier. Obsessing over daily score changes helps less than paying on time and keeping utilisation sensible.

## How to get a free copy

You can usually access free credit reports periodically directly from credit reporting bodies (processes and frequency limits apply). Prefer official bureau channels over random “free score” apps that upsell credit repair.

When the report arrives:

1. Confirm name, date of birth, and addresses.
2. Tick every account you recognise.
3. Note enquiry dates—clusters can hurt near a home-loan application.
4. Highlight anything wrong or out of date.

Keep PDFs password-protected on your device.

## Mistakes and how to dispute them

If you find an error:

- Dispute with the credit reporting body using their formal process.
- Also contact the lender or provider that supplied the data.
- Attach evidence (statements showing payments, police reports for ID theft, correspondence).
- Record reference numbers and follow up within stated timeframes.

Accurate negative information generally cannot be removed just because it is inconvenient—that is where [credit repair](/topics/loans-credit-debt/managing-debt/credit-repair) marketing often misleads. Focus on corrections and future behaviour.

If identity theft is involved, secure accounts urgently and see scams guidance; call **1800 007 007** if debts you do not recognise are driving hardship.

## Habits that improve your file over time

- Pay at least minimums on time—every time
- Reduce high revolving balances relative to limits
- Avoid unnecessary applications in a short window
- Keep old accounts in good standing when closing is optional—see [cancel a credit card](/topics/loans-credit-debt/credit-cards/cancel-a-credit-card)
- Use hardship arrangements when needed rather than ghosting creditors—arrangements can still be imperfect, but unmanaged defaults are worse

After a [loan rejection](/topics/loans-credit-debt/loans/loan-rejection), fix report issues before reapplying widely.

## How lenders actually use the file

A score is a shortcut. Underwriters and automated systems also read the raw report: recent missed payments, how many enquiries you lodged this quarter, whether limits dwarf your income, and whether employment details match your application. That is why two people with similar scores can get different outcomes.

Before a major application (home loan, large personal loan):

- freeze unnecessary new credit for a few months
- pay revolving balances down deliberately
- correct address and employment mismatches that look like identity confusion
- prepare explanations for any remaining negatives (hardship during illness, for example) if a human underwriter will see them

Do not open store cards to “build credit” right before a mortgage—extra limits and enquiries often work against borrowing capacity. If you are in hardship, focus on arrangements and essentials first; cosmetic score chasing while rent is late solves the wrong problem. Call **1800 007 007** when debts and living costs collide.

## What to do next

- Order your credit reports from the main bureaus and save copies.
- Dispute errors in writing with evidence attached.
- Build a 90-day on-time payment streak across all accounts.
- If debt stress is the real issue, pair report work with [/urgent](/urgent) and **1800 007 007**.
