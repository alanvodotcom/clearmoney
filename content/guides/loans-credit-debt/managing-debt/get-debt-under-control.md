Getting debt under control starts with a clear inventory and a repayment order you can live with. You do not need a perfect spreadsheet—just enough truth to stop guessing.

::: tip
If essentials are at risk this week, pause the spreadsheet and open [Urgent help](/urgent) first.
:::

## List every debt in one place

For each debt write who you owe, the balance, interest rate or fees, minimum repayment and due date, and what happens if you miss (home, car, utilities, unsecured). Include credit cards, personal loans, BNPL, overdue tax, Centrelink debts, family loans with expectations, and medical bills. Hidden debts sabotage plans.

If you cannot cover essentials while listing, pause and go to [/urgent](/urgent) / National Debt Helpline **1800 007 007** first—control plans fail when people are hungry or facing lockouts.

## Choose a repayment order

Two classic methods:

- **Highest rate first (avalanche)** — minimises interest cost. Use the [credit card](/tools/credit-card) and [personal loan](/tools/personal-loan) tools to see damage rates clearly.
- **Smallest balance first (snowball)** — builds momentum with quick wins.

Secured debts (mortgage, car) and debts that cut essentials (power) need special care—missing them has outsized consequences even if their interest looks moderate. Paying minimums on those while attacking high-rate unsecured debt is a common hybrid. Write the order on paper and stick it on the fridge. Changing strategy weekly wastes energy.

## Free up cash for repayments

Find money without pretending you have none: cut or pause subscriptions for 90 days, switch insurance or energy plans at renewal carefully, sell unused items, take temporary extra hours if health allows, redirect windfalls to the target debt, and negotiate lower rates or hardship variations—see [financial hardship](/topics/loans-credit-debt/managing-debt/financial-hardship). Stop adding new consumer credit while you dig out. Freeze BNPL apps and avoid store cards.

## When consolidation is and isn't the fix

Rolling debts into one loan can help if the **total cost** falls and you do not refill the old cards. It hurts when the term balloons, the rate is not truly better, or behaviour stays the same. Read [debt consolidation and refinancing](/topics/loans-credit-debt/managing-debt/debt-consolidation-and-refinancing) before you apply. If collectors are calling or legal action looms, add [financial counselling](/topics/loans-credit-debt/managing-debt/financial-counselling) rather than DIY guesswork.

## A weekly review that keeps the plan alive

Every week, spend ten minutes checking: Did the extra payment leave? Did any new BNPL or card spend appear? Is an essential bill due before the next payday? Adjust only when income changes—not when motivation dips. Share the list with a trusted person if accountability helps, or keep it private if safety requires it. Progress is usually uneven; missing one week is a reason to restart, not to abandon the project.

## Stop the leaks while you repay

Debt plans fail when new balances appear as fast as old ones shrink. Practical leak-stops: remove saved cards from shopping sites, unsubscribe from promotional emails that trigger spending, set daily transfer limits in your banking app, and keep a $20–$50 cash or debit “fun” allowance so the plan does not feel like punishment. If a partner’s spending is the leak, you need a joint conversation—or safety support—before maths can work. For card-specific tactics, see [pay off your credit card](/topics/loans-credit-debt/credit-cards/pay-off-your-credit-card). If a windfall arrives, split it deliberately: part to essentials buffer, the rest to the current target debt—do not let “treat yourself” rebuild the mountain you just climbed. When the first debt hits zero, roll its full payment onto the next one the same week so momentum does not leak into lifestyle upgrades.

## What to do next

- Complete a one-page debt list with rates and minimums.
- Pick avalanche or snowball and automate the extra payment to debt #1.
- Use [/tools/credit-card](/tools/credit-card) and [/tools/personal-loan](/tools/personal-loan) to compare payoff timelines.
- Call **1800 007 007** if essentials collide with minimum repayments.
- Revisit the debt list monthly and celebrate cleared accounts without new credit.
