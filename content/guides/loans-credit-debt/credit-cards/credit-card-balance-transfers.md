A balance transfer moves existing card debt onto a new card—often with a low or 0% promotional rate for a fixed period. Used with a written payoff plan, it can slash interest. Used as a reset button for more spending, it digs a deeper hole.

## How a 0% transfer works

You apply for a card that offers a promotional transfer rate. If approved, the new issuer pays out your old card balance (up to an allowed amount), and you repay the new card under promo rules. Key variables:

- length of the promotional period
- transfer fee (often a percentage of the amount moved)
- rate on new purchases during the promo (frequently **not** 0%)
- rate that applies when the promo ends
- whether payments reduce the transferred balance first

Transfers are not forgiveness. You still owe the money—hopefully with less interest along the way.

## Transfer fees and the real break-even

A 2% fee on a $8,000 transfer costs $160 upfront (or is added to the balance). That can still be worthwhile if you would otherwise pay more interest during the same months. It is **not** worthwhile if you only move the debt and pay minimums until the promo expires.

Do this on one page:

1. Estimate interest you would pay on the old card over the promo length.
2. Subtract the transfer fee and any annual fee on the new card.
3. Confirm you can repay the **full** transferred amount before the promo ends (or have a realistic refinance plan).

If the surplus savings are tiny, simplifying with aggressive repayment on the current card may be cleaner.

## What happens when the promo ends

On day one after the deal, remaining balances often jump to a standard purchase or cash rate that can be high. People get caught when:

- they paid only minimums during the promo
- they added new purchases that were never on 0%
- they lost track of the end date

Set reminders at 90, 30, and 14 days. If a balance will remain, contact the issuer early, consider another responsible transfer only with fresh maths, or ask about hardship. For wider debt pressure, call the National Debt Helpline on **1800 007 007** and see [/urgent](/urgent).

## Rules that keep the deal honest

- Do not use the new card for shopping while the transfer balance remains—or keep new spend on debit only.
- Close or cut up the old card once the transfer clears (after confirming the old balance is actually zero).
- Avoid transferring into a card whose post-promo rate is worse unless the timeline to zero is certain.
- Watch for multiple transfers that leave enquiry marks and higher total limits you might misuse.

A transfer is a tool inside a payoff plan—see [pay off your credit card](/topics/loans-credit-debt/credit-cards/pay-off-your-credit-card)—not a substitute for one.

## Common ways balance transfers fail

- Spending on the new card while the transferred balance remains
- Forgetting the end date and discovering a high revert rate
- Transferring only part of the debt and paying interest on what remains elsewhere
- Taking multiple sequential transfers without ever cutting the principal enough
- Ignoring the transfer fee in the “savings” story

Avoid those patterns with automation: a standing transfer sized to finish early, card locked to digital wallet only for emergencies, and a note in your phone titled “promo ends” with the real calendar date. If your income is too unstable to commit to the payoff schedule, fix cash flow with counselling before you reshuffle the debt.

## What to do next

- Calculate fee versus interest saved before you apply.
- Divide the transferred balance by months left in the promo and automate that repayment.
- Block new purchases on the transfer card.
- Diary the promo end date; if clearance is unlikely, seek help via **1800 007 007** early.
